Chapter 01

The Case of a
Hopeless Romantic

A small investigation into an old version of myself.
Personal archive / File 001Case: HR

Subject: A hopeless romantic

  • Believing in love a little too much.
  • Turning ordinary moments into meaningful memories.
  • Imagining a future before fully understanding the present.
  • Sometimes falling for what could be, rather than seeing what already was.

Case note: learning to tell the difference between a person and the version of them I created in my mind.

I used to think being a hopeless romantic was simply a beautiful thing. Maybe it was. But with time, I began to understand that sometimes I was so busy imagining what love could become that I didn't always see things as they really were.

That realisation didn't make the feelings I once had any less real. It simply taught me that loving someone also means seeing them as they are — not as a character in a story I've already written.

Out of all the things that happened, one of the memories I still hold close is something as simple as having ketoprak in Malioboro.

Chapter 03

What Life
Taught Me

The experience didn't end when the relationship did.

I've come to realise that the time we spent together was only one part of the experience. What came after — the separation, the recovery, the quiet process of putting myself back together — became another part of it.

That period gave me space to look at myself more honestly. To understand how I love, what I expect, where I fall short, and what kind of person I want to become.

I learned that memories can be beautiful without being perfect. That people are more than the versions we create in our heads. And that something doesn't have to last forever to have mattered.

I can be grateful for what happened without wishing to return to it.
See people clearly.Someone can matter deeply without being perfect or fitting an imagined ideal.
Own my part.Growing up means recognising where I could have done better.
Let memories be memories.A good moment doesn't need to become a reason to go backwards.
Keep growing.The work of healing and understanding myself belongs to me.
Chapter 04

The Things I
Never Said

An apology, and a thank you.
16 November 2026

Hi, Laurencia.

I hope life has been treating you well.

There are a few things I've wanted to say for quite some time. It took me a while to understand how to put them into words, but I think it's time I finally did.

First, I'm sorry.

Looking back, I don't think I ever truly gave you the best of what you needed or wanted from me. Maybe there were things you hoped for that I couldn't understand back then. Maybe there were moments when I could have done better, been more present, or understood you a little more.

I can't go back and change any of that. But I do want to acknowledge it. I'm sorry for the times I fell short, for the things I couldn't give, and for the ways I may have disappointed you. I wish I had understood sooner.

And alongside that apology, there's something else I want you to know.

Thank you.

Thank you for the time we shared, for the experiences we had, and for the memories that became part of my life. But my gratitude doesn't stop there. I'm also thankful for what came after we parted ways.

The time it took to recover, to reflect, to understand myself, and to slowly put the pieces together taught me things I probably wouldn't have learned otherwise. That experience became part of how I've grown into the person I am today.

I learned a lot about the way I love, the expectations I carry, the way I see people, and the person I want to become. I don't look back at everything as something perfect, but I can look back with appreciation.

I'm not writing this to bring the past back, nor am I hoping for another chance. I respect the life you've built since then. I simply wanted to say the things I should have said, without expecting anything in return.

I genuinely hope life continues to bring you good things. I hope you find happiness in the things you choose, and that the future is kind to you.

Thank you for being part of my story.
